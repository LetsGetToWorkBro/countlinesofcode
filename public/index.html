Every tool here does one job and then gets out of the way. There is nothing to sign up for, nothing to install, no free tier that runs out, and no upsell at the end. Most of them never send your file anywhere at all — the work happens in the tab you already have open.

The tools

ToolWhat it doesWhere the work happens
Count code Lines of code in any public GitHub repository, split into code, comments and blanks, per language. Comment-aware, so it does not just count newlines, and it skips node_modules and lockfiles instead of pretending they are yours. Our server, or your browser for big repositories
Code golf One stated task, fewest lines wins. Build a URL shortener or a JSON parser, count it here, and it joins that challenge's board. Every entry solves the same problem, which is the only way a line count means anything. Our server
Edit a PDF Fill in a form by typing into its actual fields, sign it by hand or by name, drop in a picture, and delete or replace text properly — click a word and it leaves the file, no black box over the top and the rest of the page still selectable. Everything you add can be dragged and resized. Your browser
PDF ↔ Word Convert a PDF into an editable Word document, or a Word document into a PDF. The only converter that tells you how well it will go before you commit — and the only one that never sees your file. Your browser
Image tools Resize, compress and convert an image between PNG, JPEG and WebP, and strip the location and camera data out of a photo. Nothing is re-uploaded to do it. Your browser
Unlock a PDF Remove the “no printing, no copying” restrictions from a PDF that opens without a password. Not a password cracker — a file that needs a password to open still needs it. Your browser
Shrink a PDF Make a heavy PDF smaller, mostly by shrinking oversized scanned pages. Shows you the before and after and lets you keep whichever is smaller. Your browser
Delete posts Bulk-delete your own history on X, Threads and Facebook Pages through the official APIs. Upload your archive, pick exactly what goes, dry run first. Our server, on your behalf

Why it looks like this

Because the alternative is what you get everywhere else. Search for “merge PDF” and every result wants your email, your file on their server, and a subscription to get it back without a watermark. The page takes four seconds to load and half of it is tracking.

So: black text on a white background, no fonts to download, no frameworks, no cookie banner because there are no cookies to warn you about. The pages are a few kilobytes and they load instantly on a bad connection. That is not nostalgia, it is just what is left when you take out the parts that were never for you.

Where a tool can run in your browser, it does. Not as a feature — as the whole point. People put contracts, payslips and passports through PDF sites without thinking about where the file went. Here there is no endpoint to send one to: turn off your wifi after the page loads and the PDF tools still work.

What it will not do

Each tool says where its own limits are, in detail, on its own page. The short version:

  • No half-working features. PDF → Word is offered, but it grades your document first and tells you plainly what will not survive, rather than shipping something that looks right and is not.
  • No numbers we cannot show our working for. The counter is documented rule by rule, pinned to a commit, and the same for everyone.
  • No accounts, so no data to lose. Connecting GitHub is optional and only exists for private repositories.
  • No ads and no tracking. There is no analytics script on any page here.

All of it is open source, including the parts that are inconvenient to admit: the toolkit and the post deleter.
