Every tool here does one job and then gets out of the way. There is nothing to sign up for, nothing to install, no free tier that runs out, and no upsell at the end. Most of them never send your file anywhere at all. The work happens in the tab you already have open.

The tools

Grouped by what you are working on. Almost everything runs in the tab you already have open; where a tool needs our server, it says so.

PDF

ToolWhat it doesRuns on
Edit a PDF Fill in a form by typing into its actual fields, sign it by hand or by name, drop in a picture, and delete or replace text properly: click a word and it leaves the file, no black box over the top and the rest of the page still selectable. Everything you add can be dragged and resized. Your browser
PDF ↔ Word Convert a PDF into an editable Word document, or a Word document into a PDF. The only converter that tells you how well it will go before you commit, and the only one that never sees your file. Scanned pages are read with optical recognition, in the tab. Your browser
Merge & split pages Combine several PDFs into one, split one apart, drag pages into a new order, turn sideways scans the right way up, and delete the pages you do not want. Pages are copied whole rather than redrawn, so the text stays selectable. Your browser
Unlock a PDF Remove the “no printing, no copying” restrictions from a PDF that opens without a password. Not a password cracker: a file that needs a password to open still needs it. Your browser
Shrink a PDF Make a heavy PDF smaller, mostly by shrinking oversized scanned pages. Shows you the before and after and lets you keep whichever is smaller. Your browser

Privacy

ToolWhat it doesRuns on
Lock a file with a password Encrypt a file before you send it, and unlock one somebody sent you. Neither the file nor the password leaves the tab. It writes the OpenPGP standard, so the person receiving it can open it with gpg rather than being tied to this page. Your browser
PGP keys and messages Make a key pair, encrypt a message or file to someone’s public key, decrypt what they send back, sign and verify. Real OpenPGP, with no keyserver and no upload. Your private key is generated in the tab and stays there. Your browser
What’s hidden in this file? Before you send a document, see what it is quietly carrying: text under black boxes that can still be copied, tracked changes, comments, author names, the company, even the timezone it was written in. Your browser
Check an email Is it really from who it says? Read the SPF, DKIM and DMARC verdicts your provider already recorded, and see the display name that says one thing while the address says another. Then see every tracking pixel and wrapped link watching you open it, named and counted and never loaded. Your browser
Temporary inbox A throwaway email address that reads its own mail, for the forms that do not deserve your real one. No account, receive only, and every message deletes itself an hour after it lands. Each one is checked for a forged sender and tracking pixels the same way the email checker is, and nothing is ever loaded or run. It is the one tool here that keeps data on a server, briefly, and says so. A little server
Monero addresses Check a Monero address before you send to it, restore a seed phrase to see which wallet it opens, or generate a paper wallet offline. The generator verifies its own arithmetic against published test vectors in front of you and refuses to run if any of it fails, and still tells you to confirm the result in the official wallet before trusting it with anything. Your browser
Monero wallet A real Monero wallet in a browser tab: create or restore one, watch the balance, receive and send. The keys stay in the tab and nothing is stored, so the seed phrase is the only copy. Node traffic is proxied through this site so the node never sees you. It is the least safe way to hold Monero and the page says so first; use it for small amounts, not savings. A node, proxied
Delete posts Bulk-delete your own history on X, Threads and Facebook Pages through the official APIs. Upload your archive, pick exactly what goes, dry run first. It is the one tool that cannot run in your browser (deleting a post means holding a token for your account and talking to X and Meta), so it lives on its own domain, where those permissions are fenced off and can never apply to anything on this one. Our server, for you

Why it looks like this

Because the alternative is what you get everywhere else. Search for “merge PDF” and every result wants your email, your file on their server, and a subscription to get it back without a watermark. The page takes four seconds to load and half of it is tracking.

So: black text on a white background, no fonts to download, no frameworks, no cookie banner because there are no cookies to warn you about. The pages are a few kilobytes and they load instantly on a bad connection. That is not nostalgia, it is just what is left when you take out the parts that were never for you.

Where a tool can run in your browser, it does. Not as a feature. It is the whole point. People put contracts, payslips and passports through PDF sites without thinking about where the file went. Here there is no endpoint to send one to: turn off your wifi after the page loads and the PDF tools still work.

What it will not do

Each tool says where its own limits are, in detail, on its own page. The short version:

  • No half-working features. PDF → Word is offered, but it grades your document first and tells you plainly what will not survive, rather than shipping something that looks right and is not.
  • No numbers we cannot show our working for. The counter is documented rule by rule, pinned to a commit, and the same for everyone.
  • No accounts, so no data to lose. Connecting GitHub is optional and only exists for private repositories.
  • No ads and no tracking. There is no analytics script on any page here.
  • The one exception is kept at arm’s length. Deleting your posts genuinely needs a server and needs permission to talk to X and Meta, so it is a separate application on a separate domain. Everything on 1999loc.com is served under a policy that forbids it contacting any other site at all, a rule your browser enforces rather than a promise from us, and that rule stays intact precisely because the tool that would need it loosened is not here. Any tool page will show you the policy and let you watch the browser refuse.

All of it is open source, including the parts that are inconvenient to admit: the toolkit and the post deleter.
