Into Monero, or back out of it, through instant exchanges.

The swap itself happens at an instant-exchange service (Exolix and Godex, plus ChangeNOW when configured), not on this site. While it runs, they hold your coins: that is custody, however brief. These services advertise no accounts and no sign-up, and for ordinary amounts that is how it goes, but their terms let them freeze a deposit their risk system dislikes and ask for identification before releasing it. It is rare. It is not impossible. Do not swap more than you would be willing to have stuck in that conversation.

Is this actually private?

  • Who does what. The exchange service does the swap and holds the coins while it runs. This site's server relays your quote and order requests so the exchange sees Cloudflare instead of your address, and stores nothing: no order table, no logs of amounts or addresses. The order id is kept in this tab and shown to you; lose both and only the exchange's own support can find your swap.
  • Both directions, one rule. Every swap here has Monero on one side, in or out. That is not a limit of the exchanges, which will trade anything for anything; it is what this page is for, and it keeps the checking down to one question with a right answer: does the address you pasted belong on the chain the money is going to. Paste a Monero address for a Tron payout and it refuses before an order exists.
  • The network matters more than the ticker. USDT on Tron and USDT on Ethereum are the same three letters and two different chains. They are listed separately for that reason, and sending one to the other's deposit address loses the money in a way nobody can undo.
  • Several desks, on purpose, and the best one is picked for you. Exolix and Godex both answer without any server-side key, so the board is a comparison out of the box rather than a single price with nothing to beat it. ChangeNOW and Trocador join them when this server has been given their keys, and Trocador is an aggregator rather than an exchange: it shops the same pair around about fifteen no-KYC desks and quotes the best of them, so one line on the board can stand for a dozen. Whichever quotes the most is selected the moment the numbers land, and you can pick a different desk if you would rather; the rate is not the only reason to choose one. None of them is an endorsement, and a desk that fails to answer costs you its quote and nothing else.
  • The estimate is an estimate. These are floating-rate swaps: what arrives is priced when your deposit confirms, not when you clicked. The difference is normally small. A fixed-rate mode exists at both services; it pays for the certainty with a worse rate, and this page does not use it.
  • Custody is real while it lasts. From the moment your deposit lands until the Monero leaves, the exchange controls the funds. Their risk systems occasionally hold a swap and ask the sender for identification, exactly the conversation Monero exists to avoid, which is why the warning above tells you not to send more than you can leave on the table.
  • Send the exact amount, once, from your own wallet. A different amount than the order states can be refunded or repriced at the exchange's discretion, a second deposit to the same address is not a second swap, and an expired order's address is dead: money sent to it afterwards is gone into a support ticket.
  • Privacy, honestly stated. A swap through a service like this is visible on both chains: the BTC or USDC you send is public forever, and the exchange knows it paid your Monero address. What you gain is that the Monero, once received, is yours to move privately. If the link between your coins and your XMR address matters to you, this convenience is not the tool for that job.
  • This page needs its server. Like the disposable inbox, this tool is an honest exception to "nothing leaves your browser": quotes and orders go through this site's Worker to the exchange, because the browser is forbidden from talking to anyone else.
